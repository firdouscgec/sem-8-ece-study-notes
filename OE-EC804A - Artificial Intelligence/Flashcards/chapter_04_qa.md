### 🎴 Card 1: GBFS vs. A* Evaluation Functions
*   **Question:** What are the mathematical evaluation functions $f(n)$ for Greedy Best-First Search and A* Search?
*   **Answer:**
    *   **Greedy Best-First:** $f(n) = h(n)$ (expands node estimated to be closest to goal).
    *   **A* Search:** $f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$ (expands node minimizing total estimated path cost).

### 🎴 Card 2: Admissible Heuristic
*   **Question:** Define an **admissible** heuristic mathematically.
*   **Answer:** A heuristic $h(n)$ is admissible if it never overestimates the actual cost to reach the goal from node $n$.
    $$0 \le h(n) \le h^*(n)$$
    Where $h^*(n)$ is the true optimal cost.

---

### 🎴 Card 3: Consistent Heuristic
*   **Question:** Define a **consistent** (monotonic) heuristic mathematically.
*   **Answer:** A heuristic $h(n)$ is consistent if it satisfies the triangle inequality:
    $$h(n) \le c(n, a, n') + h(n')$$
    Where $n'$ is the successor of $n$ generated by action $a$, and $c(n, a, n')$ is the step cost.

---

### 🎴 Card 4: Consistent vs. Admissible
*   **Question:** Does consistency imply admissibility, or vice versa?
*   **Answer:** Consistency **always** implies admissibility. However, an admissible heuristic is not necessarily consistent (though most practical ones are).

### 🎴 Card 5: Simulated Annealing Acceptance Probability
*   **Question:** In Simulated Annealing, what is the probability of accepting a worse move ($\Delta E \le 0$)?
*   **Answer:**
    $$P = e^{\frac{\Delta E}{T}}$$
    Where $\Delta E$ is the change in energy/value, and $T$ is the current temperature. As $T \rightarrow 0$, the probability of accepting worse moves drops to 0.

### 🎴 Card 6: Limitations of Hill-Climbing
*   **Question:** What are the three major limitations of basic Hill-Climbing search?
*   **Answer:**
    1.  **Local Maxima:** Peaks higher than neighbors but lower than global maximum.
    2.  **Ridges:** Sequences of local maxima causing oscillation.
    3.  **Plateaus:** Flat areas where all neighbors have the same value, causing random walks.

---

### 🎴 Card 7: Local Beam Search k-value
*   **Question:** How does Local Beam Search operate, and how does it differ from Random-Restart Hill-Climbing?
*   **Answer:**
    *   **Operation:** Tracks $k$ states in parallel. At each step, it generates all successors, and selects the $k$ best to continue.
    *   **Difference:** Unlike independent random restarts, beam search threads share information. Successful threads recruit resources, while poor ones are pruned.

---

### 🎴 Card 8: AND-OR Graph Search
*   **Question:** What is an AND-OR graph, and what algorithm is designed to search it?
*   **Answer:**
    *   **AND-OR Graph:** A graph representing problems that can be solved by choosing alternative paths (OR branches) or by decomposing a problem into sub-problems that must *all* be solved (AND branches).
    *   **Algorithm:** **AO* Search** (uses heuristic estimates and updates sub-tree costs backward).
